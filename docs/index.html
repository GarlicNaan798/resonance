Five minutes · no account · nothing recorded about you

Can you tell which headline won?

You will see 30 pairs of real headlines. Each pair ran as a genuine randomised A/B test on the same article, at the same moment, to the same audience, so exactly one of them really did get more clicks.

Your job is to pick which. That is the whole study, about five minutes. At the end you can stop, or carry on with 30 more if you are enjoying it.

Start the study

Why it is worth five minutes

We built a model that predicts which headline wins. It scores 61.8% against a 50% coin flip. What nobody knows, including us, is whether that is any better than a person who writes copy for a living.

This settles it. The model answers the same pairs you do, and the two get compared directly. The result gets published either way, including the outcome where people beat the model.

Please do not look anything up. The headlines are from a public dataset, so the answers are findable if you try. We are measuring judgement, not search skill, and a realistic result is far more useful to us than a flattering one.

What is recorded

  • Your choices, and how long you spent on each
  • Roughly how many years you have worked with marketing copy
  • Whether copywriting is part of your paid work

That is everything. No name, no email, no IP address, no cookies, no analytics. The page makes no network requests at all, open your browser's network tab and check. Nothing is transmitted anywhere: at the end you get a block of text, and you decide whether to send it back.

The study design, if you care

Pre-registered before any participant existed, including what each possible result would mean, so the outcome cannot be reinterpreted afterwards into whatever flatters us. The winner appears first in exactly 30 of the 60 items, so always picking the top option scores 50% by construction, and the pairs come in a different order for every person.
